## 1. Executive Summary

The methodology presented in the thesis (`03Seccion03.tex`, `metodologia.tex`, `metodologia_temporal/*.md` and `reproducibility.md`) demonstrates a **high level of technical rigor** in its implementation but requires **stronger narrative bridging** between the formal mathematical definitions and the specific experimental design decisions.

The computational model is robust, with a clear pipeline (Generation $\to$ Feature Extraction $\to$ Dimensionality Reduction $\to$ Visualization). The switch to a "Combinatorial + Structural" generation approach (`modelo_computacional...md`) addresses previous limitations of database-dependency. The experimental validation (`04Seccion04.tex`) follows a logical progression from atomic units (dyads) to massive populations (100k chords), providing strong visual evidence of the model's consistency.

**Key Strengths:**
*   **Logical Progression:** Experiments 1-6 build complexity systematically.
*   **Reproducibility:** The `REPRODUCIBILITY.md` and CLI tools (`tools.run_lab`) show a commitment to open science best practices.
*   **Formalization:** The mathematical model of roughness (Sethares) and chord representation is well-defined.

**Key Weaknesses:**
*   **Validation Gaps:** While visual clusters are interpreted musically, there is a lack of *quantitative* ground-truth validation (e.g., comparing model distances against a perceptual dataset or a music theory corpus).
*   **Justification of Parameters:** The choice of dimensionality reduction parameters (MDS vs UMAP, n_neighbors, perplexity) needs more explicit justification in the methodology chapter itself, rather than just reporting results.
*   **Substitution Metric Naïveté:** The current substitution proposal (JSD + Jaccard) is acknowledged as a "first pass" (`cuestiones_sustitucion...md`). The thesis needs to explicitly frame this as a meaningful limitation/scope boundary vs. a finalized solution.

## 2. ScholarEval Framework Analysis

### 2.1 Problem Formulation (Score: 4/5)
*   **Definition:** The problem of quantifying chord utilization and similarity is well-defined mathematically ($S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{12}$).
*   **Significance:** The separation between "structural" (PC-set) and "sensorial" (Roughness) properties is a strong theoretical contribution.
*   **Gap:** The specific "why" for *harmonic substitution* needs to be tied better to the *roughness* metric. Why should roughness similarity imply functional substitution? This link is assumed but not proven.

### 2.2 Methodology & Approach (Score: 4.5/5)
*   **Design:** The 6-experiment roadmap (`fig:roadmap_experimentos`) is excellent. It isolates variables (cardinality, register, doubling) effective.
*   **Rigor:** The use of `run_lab` and `compare_sethares` scripts ensures the computational implementation matches the theoretical claims.
*   **Reproducibility:** `REPRODUCIBILITY.md` provides clear entry points. The dual-path (SQL vs Combinatorial) is a bit confusing in documentation but functional in code.

### 2.3 Analysis & Results (Score: 4/5)
*   **Evidence:** The "roadmap" of experiments provides strong *qualitative* evidence. The stability analysis (`fig:fiabilidad_sensibilidad`) adds necessary *quantitative* confidence to the dimensionality reduction.
*   **Interpretation:** The musical interpretation of clusters (e.g., "MDS attracts rough chords to center") is consistent.
*   **Missing:** A "Confusion Matrix" or similar metric for the "known structures" experiment. How many "Major Triads" ended up in the "Diminished" cluster? A quantitative error rate would strengthen Exp 2.

### 2.4 Writing & Communication (Score: 3.5/5)
*   **Clarity:** The text is generally clear but oscillates between high-level musical concepts and low-level code details without a smooth transition.
*   **Structure:** `03Seccion03.tex` mixes mathematical definitions with experimental outcomes. It might be cleaner to separate "The Model" (pure math/algo) from "The Experiments" (validation).
*   **Visuals:** The diagrams generated by the pipeline are high quality. The "Roadmap" diagram in `04Seccion04.tex` is critical and should be refined for publication.

## 3. Scientific Critical Thinking Analysis

### 3.1 Evidence Quality Assessment (GRADE-like)
*   **Directness:** **High.** The experiments directly measure what the model calculates (geometric distances vs. theoretical expectations).
*   **Consistency:** **High.** Results across MDS and UMAP (`tab:resumen_metricas_simple`) show consistent high trustworthiness (>0.95 usually).
*   **Precision:** **Moderate.** The "hit rate" drops significantly in Exp 5 (Random Sample), suggesting the model struggles with unstructured noise or that the concept of "neighbor" becomes fuzzy in high density. This limitation should be discussed honestly.

### 3.2 Bias Detection
*   **Selection Bias:** The "Known Structures" (Exp 2) are cherry-picked from music theory. This is necessary for validation but introduces bias. **Recommendation:** Explicitly state that Exp 2 is a "Sanity Check" on known data, not a test of discovery power.
*   **Confirmation Bias:** The musical interpretation of the UMAP clusters ("natural separation of Major/Minor") aligns with expectation. Did the author look for *counter-examples*? Are there Major triads that were projected far away? Discussing outliers would reduce confirmation bias.

### 3.3 Alternative Explanations
*   **Artifacts of Dimensionality Reduction:** The "center attraction" in MDS might be an artifact of high-dimensional "hollowness" projected to 2D, rather than just "roughness". The stability analysis helps, but comparing against a PCA baseline (linear) could prove the non-linearity is required.
*   **Sethares Parameter Sensitivity:** Does the roughness topology change drastically if $f_{min}$ changes? `REPRODUCIBILITY.md` suggests checking this. The thesis should briefly mention if the results are robust to parameter tuning (sensitivity analysis).
