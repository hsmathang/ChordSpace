# Hypotheses for Theoretical Framework

Based on the thesis proposal ("Propuesta_Trabajo_Master_vf-3-Copy.pdf") and the adopted methodology ("capitulo_metodologia.tex"), the theoretical framework must support the following core hypotheses:

## General Hypothesis (H0)
> "Dado un contexto armónico definido existen acordes no explorados que satisfacen características de similitud sonora que los habilitan como sustitutos de otros acordes conocidos."
> *(Given a defined harmonic context, there exist unexplored chords that satisfy sound similarity characteristics enabling them as substitutes for known chords.)*

## Specific Hypotheses for the Theoretical Framework

### H1: Psychoacoustic Viability as a Proxy for Harmonic Function
**Hypothesis:** The mathematical formalization of sensory dissonance (specifically, Plomp-Levelt and Sethares' roughness models) provides a quantifiable metric of "sound similarity" that strongly correlates with the cognitive and cultural perception of chord substitution viability in isolated contexts.
**Theoretical Need:** The framework must explain *why* roughness is a valid proxy. It must bridge the gap between low-level psychoacoustics (critical bands, partials) and high-level music theory (consonance, harmonic function).

### H2: Topological Structure vs. Machine Learning "Black Boxes"
**Hypothesis:** An explicitly defined topological space (using deterministic interval-class vectors and roughness matrices) yields a more interpretable and analytically rigorous representation of chord relationships compared to latent spaces generated by deep learning embeddings (e.g., Forte, Chord2Vec).
**Theoretical Need:** The framework must contrast our mathematical/topological approach with current machine learning state-of-the-art. It must highlight the "explicability" advantage of our model—why knowing *exactly* why two chords are close is better for a musician than a deep learning recommendation.

### H3: Contextual Independence (Isolated Chords vs. Progressions)
**Hypothesis:** The inherent tension and similarity between chords can be evaluated in isolation (vertical harmony), providing a foundational "substitution space" that exists prior to the application of horizontal/syntactic rules (voice leading, progressions like in Baroque music).
**Theoretical Need:** The framework must clearly delineate the scope of the thesis: we are modeling the intrinsic sonic properties of isolated chords sets, not predicting the next chord in a temporal sequence.
